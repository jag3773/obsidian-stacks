# What Stacks does

## Every format your library comes in

Not every library is a folder of PDFs. Yours might be ebooks bought over the years, Kindle books, a shelf of Kobo downloads, or reports that arrive as Word files. Stacks files them all the same way.

| Drop in | Stacks reads |
|---|---|
| **PDF** | A DOI, ISBN or PubMed ID in the first pages, and the document's title and author; without them, the byline under the title (author and date), and for a web page printed from a browser, its URL and the day it was printed |
| **EPUB**, including **Kobo** `.kepub` | The book's own ISBN, title, authors, publisher and date, or the ISBN on its copyright page |
| **Kindle** `.mobi`, `.azw`, `.azw3` | The ISBN, title, authors, publisher and date in the book's header, which is readable even when the book has DRM |
| **Word** `.docx` and **OpenDocument** `.odt` | The title and author, and a DOI or ISBN in the text |

- **Identified, not just renamed.** Stacks looks each file up by its ISBN or DOI, and the file's own details fill whatever the lookup lacks. When nothing is found, the file alone is enough to start a reference: a book for an ebook, a document otherwise.
- **One reference, every copy.** Drop the EPUB of a book you already have and it joins that reference, beside the PDF. Every file a source has lives in its `attachments` list: the book, a chapter scan, the slides that came with a talk.
- **Your format comes first.** Set your **Preferred formats** (`pdf, epub` by default, or `epub, pdf` for an ebook library). A reference's main file is its attachment in your first format. The library base's **File** column opens it, and its **Missing file** view shows what has none.
- **Kept healthy.** **Check library health** finds broken links and unlinked files in every format. Its repair relinks an ebook only to an ebook and a PDF only to a PDF, after a preview.

Highlights and the tablet round trip work with PDFs today. Upgrading from 0.4? Notes with the old `pdf` property keep working unchanged, and **Repair attachment links** moves it into `attachments` after a preview. See [files and the main file](attachments.md).

## Available today

- **Save a source by identifier.** Paste an ISBN, DOI, arXiv ID, or URL. Stacks looks up metadata, checks for an existing reference, creates a note, and opens it.
- **Turn an inbox into a library.** Drop in a Web Clipper note, a PDF, an ebook (EPUB, Kobo or Kindle) or a Word or OpenDocument file. Clipped notes are enriched and filed; what Web Clipper saved (author, published date, description) fills anything the lookup can't find, which covers sites that turn Stacks away. Looked-up values replace the guesses Web Clipper made from the page, such as its page title; the URL and status are kept. Files are checked for an ISBN or DOI, in an ebook's own metadata or a PDF's opening pages, paired with a reference note, and moved into your attachments folder. An ebook of a book you already have is attached to it. When a lookup cannot identify a file, its own metadata or filename provides a starting point. See [files and the main file](attachments.md#dropping-files-in-the-inbox).
- **Keep files in any format.** A reference's PDF, ebook and other files all go in its `attachments`. Its main file is the one in your most preferred format, `pdf, epub` by default, so an ebook library works as well as a PDF one. See [files and the main file](attachments.md).
- **Clip from the browser.** Obsidian Web Clipper is the main way in; see [capturing with Web Clipper](web-clipper.md) for the properties Stacks reads and a template to import. PubMed records and IDs (`PMID: 12345678`) and COinS on catalog and repository pages are read too.
- **Find an open-access PDF.** **Find PDF for current reference** checks Unpaywall and the work's landing page, shows which copy and version it found, and attaches it only after you confirm. See [Find PDF](find-pdf.md).
- **Complete a reference as you write.** Add an ISBN, DOI, or URL property to a note in your references folder. Stacks can fill missing metadata and name the note for you. Metadata enrichment preserves populated properties and the existing note body.
- **Start with the details that matter.** References come with a title, authors, publication date (`published`), year, abstract, and, where the source has them, publisher, ISBN, or DOI. Book pages on Amazon, Goodreads, and Kobo, GitHub repositories, and news and blog articles are read the way Zotero's translators read them.
- **Add what no lookup can find.** **New reference without a lookup** takes a type, title, authors and date and creates the note and its citekey offline: talks, interviews, older books, internal documents. **Refresh metadata** looks a reference up again and shows each changed field to accept or skip.
- **Keep complete citation data.** Editors, translators, volume, issue, pages, edition, place, series, language, ISSN and access dates are kept when the source has them, each mapped to your own property names. See [citation fields](citation-fields.md).
- **Export a bibliography.** CSL-JSON for Pandoc and Quarto, or BibLaTeX or BibTeX, for the whole library or a filtered project, written on demand or automatically whenever references change.
- **Cite while you write.** **Insert citation** searches your references by citekey, title, author or year and inserts `[@citekey]`, `[[citekey]]` or both, with an optional page. **Copy formatted citation** and **Copy formatted bibliography** put a citation or reference list on the clipboard in Chicago author-date or any CSL style in your vault, italics included. A draft's bibliography covers the references it cites. See [citing your sources](citing.md), including the path from a note to a finished document with Pandoc or Quarto.
- **Move from Zotero.** **Import from Zotero** reads a Better BibTeX JSON export, previews what it will do, adds properties to your existing notes without touching their bodies, and can be undone. See [migrating from Zotero](migrating-from-zotero.md).
- **Import bibliography files.** **Import bibliography file** reads CSL-JSON, BibTeX, BibLaTeX, or RIS with the same preview, per-record problem report, and undo. Citekeys from the file are kept, and importing the file again updates the same notes instead of duplicating them. See [importing bibliography files](importing.md).
- **Give sources useful citekeys.** Use a configurable Better BibTeX-style formula, with collision handling. The default is `auth.lower + shorttitle(3, 3) + year`. Insert them as you write with **Insert citation**.
- **Browse with Obsidian Bases.** Choose your library's `.base` file, or create a starter Base. Match fields to your vault's property names and browse your collection with tables, filters, and reading-status views.
- **Turn highlights into notes.** Run **Extract annotations** on a reference and its PDF's highlights, underlines, and comments appear in the note as callouts grouped by page, each linked to its page. Colors map to callout types you choose. Re-extracting updates only Stacks' marked block, so your own writing in the note is never touched. On desktop, [Parabloom](https://github.com/jag3773/parabloom) can first expand highlights that cover only a few words to the whole paragraph. See [Highlights, comments and the reading round trip](annotations.md#expand-highlights-to-whole-paragraphs-with-parabloom).
- **Bring in Kindle highlights.** With the [Kindle Highlights](https://github.com/hadynz/obsidian-kindle-plugin) plugin writing into a folder inside your references folder, each Kindle book becomes one reference: Stacks completes the note (by ISBN, or by title and author) or adds its highlights to the reference you already have, and the Kindle plugin keeps adding new highlights to it. See [Kindle highlights](kindle.md).
- **Read on a tablet and bring your highlights home (desktop).** Point Stacks at a folder your reading app syncs. Setting a note to `reading` sends the PDF there; when it comes back changed, Stacks files it, sets the status to `reviewing`, and extracts the annotations. If the PDF also changed in the vault, you choose which copy to keep, and the other is saved beside it.
- **Keep the library healthy.** **Check library health** writes a report note linking to each problem: missing or duplicate citekeys, broken attachment links, files no note links to, broken frontmatter, items stuck in a status, and records missing what their citation needs. Repairs, such as relinking a file named after the citekey or merging exact duplicates, are separate commands that show a preview first. See [library health and duplicates](maintenance.md).
- **Make it fit your vault.** Configure folders, filenames, property mappings, note templates, statuses, and author links. Folder and template settings suggest matching vault paths as you type.
- **Edit many references at once.** Set a status, add or remove a collection, regenerate citekeys, or complete metadata for a selection, a folder, a collection's members, or the rows of a **Stacks batch** view in a Base, always after a preview. **Stacks: Create base** writes views for each reading status, unfiled references and references missing a file. See [collections, library views and batch edits](batch-and-collections.md).
- **Capture offline, sync anywhere.** A clip saved without a connection waits in the inbox and is retried later; duplicate checks see notes that just arrived through sync, so an item isn't filed twice. See [sync, backups and mobile](sync-and-mobile.md) and [searching your library](searching.md).
